# 15. CV screening and shortlisting: user guide

Phase 6 helps the recruitment team decide **who to take forward**. GRSMS scores every application against the vacancy's requirements and ranks them. The recruiter and the department's **Head of Department** then agree a shortlist together.

## 1. Screening (automatic)

Every application is scored out of **100** the moment it is submitted:

| Part | Points | How |
|---|---|---|
| Education | 20 | Meets the vacancy's minimum level = 20; one level below = 10; lower = 0 |
| Experience | 20 | In proportion to the minimum years (full marks at or above the minimum) |
| Essential skills and certifications | 45 | Share of the vacancy's *essential* items the applicant lists |
| Desirable skills and certifications | 15 | Share of the *desirable* items the applicant lists |

A part the vacancy leaves empty (for example no desirable items) gives full marks.

**Meets the minimum** (✓) = the education and experience minimums are met **and every essential item is found**.

**How "found" works:** an item counts when all its words appear in what the applicant entered as skills, certifications, field of study or job title. Case and punctuation are ignored. For example, *NEBOSH IGC* is found in *NEBOSH International General Certificate (IGC)*.

**What screening does not do:**
* It does not read the text inside CV files; it uses what applicants entered in their profile. That is why the profile asks for skills and certifications one per line.
* It does not decide. The score ranks and highlights; people decide. Always open the CV of anyone near the line.

A reviewer can click **Re-run screening** on the board at any time.

## 2. The shortlisting board

Open a vacancy and click **Shortlisting** (or use the link on any application page). The board shows:
* the vacancy's requirements (what the screening checks);
* every application **ranked**: those meeting the minimum first, then by score, then by who applied first;
* each applicant's score bar, missing essentials, the recruiter's and HOD's recommendations, and status.

## 3. Shortlisting, step by step

```
Recruiter recommends ──► Head of Department gives their view ──► Recruiter or HR Manager shortlists
   (shortlist / do not)        (agree / disagree + reason)          (HR Manager only, with a reason,
                                                                      if the HOD has not agreed)
```

1. **The Recruitment Officer (or HR Manager) recommends.** For each applicant, open **Recruiter recommendation** and choose **Shortlist** or **Do not shortlist**. *Do not shortlist* needs a note for the HOD. A newly received application moves to *Under review* automatically.
2. **Ask the HOD.** Click **Ask the HOD for their view**. The department's Head of Department is emailed, and the request appears in their *My tasks* on the Home page.
3. **The Head of Department gives their view** on the board: **Agree: shortlist** or **Do not shortlist**. If they disagree with the recruiter, a comment is required. Only the Head of *that* department can do this.
4. **Shortlist.** Once the HOD has agreed, the Recruitment Officer or HR Manager clicks **Shortlist**. The applicant is emailed, and their tracker moves to **Shortlisted**.
   * If the HOD has **not agreed** (or not answered), only an **HR Manager** can shortlist, by typing the reason. This is recorded as an *override*.

Applicants who are not taken forward are closed with **Mark not successful** on the application page (Phase 5). The internal reason is never shown to the applicant.

**Changing your mind:** if the recruiter changes a recommendation, the HOD's earlier view is cleared and they are asked again.

**Safeguards:**
* Nobody recommends or shortlists their own application.
* HODs only see their own departments' boards.
* Every recommendation, view, shortlist and override is in the application's history and the audit trail.
